# PART I — What we may offer Argentina

## Core recommendation

Run one bounded Argentina capacity pilot asking how fiscal stability can be protected while more of the country's existing human, educational, cultural, productive and natural capacity is activated to improve everyday life and future income.

## Why investigate this

Argentina already has valuable capabilities in education, research, knowledge services, regional production, food, culture, tourism, craft and natural diversity.

The investigation does not assume that public spending should simply increase. It asks a harder question:

**Where is spending genuinely inefficient, where could short-term saving destroy valuable future capacity, and where could better use and connection of existing Argentine capacity create more value?**

## Main fields

### Education, research and work
Examine the whole capacity chain from school and vocational learning through universities and research to meaningful work, enterprise and public-purpose activity.

### Knowledge economy and exports
Identify capabilities Argentina can sell internationally while preserving the educational and research capacity that makes those exports possible.

### Domestic value creation
Look for real Argentine needs that Argentine productive capacity can meet: housing, repair, mobility, food, learning, culture, care, energy services and regional infrastructure.

### Local refinement
Test where more value can remain locally through food, furniture, craft, textiles, repair, hospitality, processing and small industry.

### Housing and life-course capacity
Ask whether housing, transport, education and work opportunity can create realistic pathways for people to build viable lives, without the state planning individual lives.

### Welfare as societal capacity
Investigate welfare as:

- safety net;
- capacity restoration;
- long-term social investment;
- institutional role-modelling of solidarity and dignity;
- possible initiative-enabling security when people know a setback does not mean abandonment.

These are testable hypotheses, not an assumption that more spending is always better.

### Preserve the good; improve the carrier
Start by identifying what actually improves human life. Then ask whether later material, chemical, packaging or production choices add little additional value while shifting toxicity, waste or cost to others, ecosystems or the future.

## Fundamental investigation lens

When an efficiency measure, reform, cut or technological change is presented as an improvement, ask:

1. What actually improves?
2. For whom?
3. Which valued function is preserved or weakened?
4. Which capacities are created, preserved or lost?
5. Are costs shifted to other people, institutions, ecosystems or future generations?
6. Which alternatives preserve the good function with better total consequences?
7. What evidence would weaken our leading interpretation?

## Demonstration product

### Argentine Living Culture & Discovery Atlas

Pilot 5–10 very different Argentine places. Build living portraits that connect food, local production, art, knowledge, history, nature, activities, accommodation, events and transport.

Do not scale merely because the concept is attractive. Compare it against existing Argentine services. Continue only if it reveals materially useful cultural, educational or productive capacity that is currently hard to discover or connect.

## What our institute could contribute

- bounded societal-capacity analysis;
- public-document and evidence synthesis;
- accountability mapping;
- alternative-scenario design;
- local-capacity discovery;
- pilot/service architecture;
- agent-assisted research and knowledge organisation;
- implementation support;
- training and capability development;
- outcome evaluation and learning.

Journalism remains independent from paid follow-on work. Analysis does not inherit democratic mandate.

## Recommended first action

One bounded pilot with Argentine evidence, institutions and local participants, predefined success/failure criteria and an independent negative review.

If it reveals no material added value, stop.

# PART II — A guide Argentina itself could give to people

## Working concept

### Discover Argentina — and what Argentines make possible

A physical and digital national guide helping people discover more of their own country: places, people, knowledge, food, craft, art, nature and opportunities to learn and participate.

It is not primarily a tourism catalogue and should not be propaganda.

## The invitation

**See what we have.  
Discover where it is.  
Meet people who know and make things.  
Taste, listen, learn and explore.  
Travel further in your own country.  
Support worthwhile local production.  
Create something yourself.  
Help build what comes next.**

## The two connected objects

### The physical booklet

A beautiful, practical object people can hold, keep, lend, annotate and take with them.

### The living digital atlas

The continuously updated layer: maps, schedules, routes, accessibility, deeper stories, local producers, learning opportunities and corrections.

QR codes and simple place identifiers connect paper and digital layers.

## What a place portrait can reveal

**Food:** dishes, markets, farms, cafés, restaurants, ingredients and seasons.

**Craft and production:** furniture, woodworking, ceramics, textiles, leather, metal, instruments, repair and small manufacturing.

**Arts and culture:** dance, music, theatre, cinema, literature, art, architecture, festivals and living traditions.

**Learning:** universities, vocational schools, libraries, lectures, workshops, short courses, research and local experts.

**History and people:** local and migration history, notable people, oral heritage and Indigenous histories with proper participation and provenance.

**Nature:** mountains, forests, steppe, wetlands, coast, rivers, deserts, thermal springs, wildlife, geology and dark skies.

**Activity:** skiing, walking, cycling, riding, paddling, climbing, bathing and accessible family activity where appropriate.

**Stay:** small hotels, guesthouses, rural stays and locally rooted hospitality.

**Calendar:** performances, festivals, lectures, markets, harvests, exhibitions, food seasons, sport and community events.

**Travel onward:** train, bus, bicycle, electric mobility, scenic routes and worthwhile neighbouring places.

**Bring home:** durable local goods, foods, books, art and craft.

**Local needs:** skills, restoration, preservation, training, infrastructure or civic opportunities that the locality itself chooses to make visible.

**Respect:** ecological limits, carrying capacity, protected areas, cultural guidance, accessibility and safety.

## Distribution is part of the product

If this exists only as a PDF on a government website, this part of the concept has failed.

Put the physical guide where people already are:

- schools and universities;
- libraries;
- railway and bus stations;
- municipal and provincial offices;
- cultural centres and museums;
- tourism offices;
- community organisations;
- selected public-service locations;
- bookstores and locally rooted cafés/businesses;
- national and regional events.

It must reach provinces and smaller communities, not only Buenos Aires.

## The economic hypothesis to test

**visibility -> curiosity -> domestic travel and learning -> local spending/participation -> stronger local producers and cultural institutions -> more reasons to discover a region -> stronger regional capacity**

This is a hypothesis, not a promised result.

Measure local value, discovery, participation, transport patterns, local satisfaction and ecological pressure.

## Editorial safeguards

Invitation, not propaganda.  
Communities are not tourism products.  
Payment does not buy editorial inclusion.  
Local/traditional is not automatically better.  
Popular/new is not automatically better.  
Culture and Indigenous/local knowledge require provenance and participation.  
Do not maximise visitor volume at the expense of what made the place valuable.

## First pilot

Choose 5–10 culturally and geographically different places. Build the complete place portraits with local sources. Compare them with existing guides and services.

Scale only if the prototype makes valuable culture, learning, local production or connections materially easier to discover without excessive local or ecological burden.
