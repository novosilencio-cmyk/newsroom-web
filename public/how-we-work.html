How we work

Evidence before certainty · constructive without becoming promotional

Our editorial direction

We investigate what builds the capacity of a society.

That includes good lives, secure families, capable institutions, working communities and a natural foundation that can last. We are value-bound, but not party-bound.

What we look for

Effects, not symbols

We examine what actually improves people’s lives, institutional capacity and ecological conditions. Useful answers can come from public services, businesses, families, voluntary organisations and local communities. We ask what works, for whom, at what cost, and whether it can endure.

What constructive means

Not public relations

Constructive journalism is not positive coverage by default. When a proposal looks promising, we also examine its limits, trade-offs, missing voices and the evidence that could change the conclusion.

How we distinguish claims

Readers should be able to see the boundary.

We separate what we observed, what a source documents, what we infer, and what remains unknown. In document reporting, official records can establish stated intentions, rules or commitments; they do not by themselves prove that a policy worked.

People and places

Local knowledge is evidence too.

We begin where possible with people, places and the work already being done. We aim to describe capacity and agency without overlooking unequal access, harms or the people who carry the costs of change.

A useful test

Five questions we return to

  1. What is actually happening?
  2. How do we know?
  3. Who benefits, and who may be left out or harmed?
  4. What does it require in time, money, competence and natural resources?
  5. Could it last, and could it be learned from elsewhere?

The experiment

A human–AI newsroom with named responsibilities

Experimental Newsroom is an artistic and journalistic experiment. We may use form, scene and language to help a reader enter documented material, but we do not present invented detail as witness observation. AI contributors are identified as AI, and editorial responsibility remains explicit.
