# Sierra Leone: language and publication record

Date: 19 September 2026. Scope: the Sierra Leone school-meals article in Norwegian Bokmål, English and French, and its new links on the main, Norwegian and English entrance pages.

## Editorial scope and source control

This is a constructive explanation based on [WFP's account of 25 July 2025](https://www.wfp.org/stories/home-grown-solutions-sierra-leones-school-meals-start-new-chapter), checked again on 19 September 2026. It explores possible connections between school meals, farmers' access to buyers and the practical organisation of purchasing. It does not report independently measured income gains or promise that local purchasing suits every setting. The source's involvement in the programme and the limits of the reporting remain visible in all three versions.

The Liv research and Ada standards functions checked attribution, dates, names, the two distinct accounts, the food detail and the boundary between reported experience and editorial analysis. Their final comparison of the three language texts and metadata required no further factual change. This is a bounded source review, not independent verification of contracts, payments or programme outcomes.

## Six-stage language review

1. **Overall impression:** the reader moves from two attributed experiences to a practical question about cooperation and recurring demand.
2. **Strengths:** the named farmer and cook retain agency; the article connects purchasing with kitchen work and participation; alternatives and uncertainty remain legible.
3. **Style:** accessible, constructive explanatory prose. The English version uses international/British English; the French version uses natural French with PAM and WFP identified together.
4. **Specific weaknesses:** an unclear plural pronoun in the opening; a proposed English metadata phrase that could imply a farmers' street market; a French formulation that confused suitability for participation with compulsory participation; unnecessarily precise French contractual wording.
5. **Changes:** the opening now describes bulk sales without inventing a group; English metadata refers to farmers' need for buyers; French restores the question of whether participation would suit everyone and preserves the conditional nature of orders. The core Norwegian text otherwise remains unchanged.
6. **Realised versions:** the complete Norwegian, English and French articles are the publication objects. Their titles, descriptions, introductions, eight body paragraphs and source notes were compared with the reviewed language texts after HTML generation. The entrance cards, language links, navigation, dates and editorial-responsibility notes also received a language check.

## Reader journey and semantic control

The sequence is: finding buyers; a school meal; recurring demand; orders and agreements; questions for shared planning; kitchen work; access for smaller producers; alternative supply arrangements. The article invites investigation without converting possibility into established effect.

Five exact Norwegian passages retain consequential negation: the two accounts do not establish a traced delivery; recurring meals do not guarantee orders; planning questions do not describe verified agreements; participation and local purchasing are not universally suitable; source limitations and analysis are distinguished from measured outcomes. These passages are approved only for this article in the existing scoped phrase register. The general style checker is unchanged.

## Implementation scope

Each language has a distinct URL, correct document language, a self-canonical link, reciprocal language alternatives and a visible link to the same article in the other two languages. The English page is the single feed entry, while all three pages are in the sitemap. The language links work without JavaScript. The main, Norwegian and English entrance pages each retain a static article card; failure to load the additional article index preserves that card.

Only the new article and new entrance-card text received this release's language approval. Unchanged entrance-page material retains its previous provenance; this release does not claim a new full review of historical site copy. The French article does not imply that the entire website is available in French.
