# Argentina — city batch pilot architecture 001

Status: INTERNAL / NOT PUBLISHED / NOT SENT
Purpose: distribute attention across multiple smaller Argentine localities rather than concentrating promotion on one or two places.

## Core rule

Do not build Mi Argentina around a single showcase town. Use balanced batches so attention, travel demand and local learning are distributed geographically and culturally.

Trevelin is the first demonstrator, not the sole model.

## Batch size

Default working unit: 10 localities.

Ten is large enough to show national diversity and reduce over-concentration, but small enough for field teams, editorial review, local participation and ecological/carrying-capacity checks to remain serious.

Each batch should be released/presented as a set rather than sequentially spotlighting only one town for long periods.

## Batch 1 — proposed 10-place demonstrator

1. Trevelin, Chubut — anchor demonstrator; Welsh-Patagonian history, local food, craft, nature, learning and production.
2. Gaiman, Chubut — cultural continuity, food/tea traditions, local identity and history; useful comparison with Trevelin inside the same province.
3. Villa Tulumba, Córdoba — historic built environment, craft, rural culture and small-place preservation.
4. Maimará, Jujuy — Quebrada landscape, agriculture, wine, Indigenous and regional cultural continuity.
5. Colonia Carlos Pellegrini, Corrientes — wetlands/ecotourism, local community, nature stewardship and carrying-capacity questions.
6. Seclantás, Salta — textile/craft traditions, Calchaquí culture and locally rooted production.
7. Famatina, La Rioja — mountain landscape, agriculture, local production, history and environmental context.
8. Saldungaray, Buenos Aires — small-town heritage, architecture, regional food/production and proximity to larger population centres.
9. Zenón Pereyra, Santa Fe — agricultural/productive context, immigration heritage, architecture, local celebrations and automotive history.
10. El Trapiche, San Luis — river/nature setting, local recreation, regional accessibility and quality-of-life potential.

## Why these 10

The batch intentionally mixes:
- Patagonia, northwest, northeast, Cuyo/central west, central pampas and Córdoba;
- nature-heavy and culture-heavy places;
- already-recognised and less globally visible localities;
- food, craft, history, agriculture, landscape, architecture and social/festival life;
- different likely carrying capacities.

Selection is provisional. Local willingness, ecological limits, accessibility, field feasibility and whether a guide would add material value must be checked before activation.

## Verified scale signal

Argentina's 2026 Best Tourism Villages national process received 56 applications from 19 provinces. The programme's stated eligibility criteria include low population density and a maximum of 15,000 inhabitants for participating villages. This does not prove that Argentina has only 56 or exactly 56 Trevelin-scale localities; it does verify a ready-made national pool of dozens of small rural-tourism localities that can be investigated as candidate sources for Mi Argentina.

Therefore the working assumption is not 'find ten and stop'. It is:

BATCH 1 (10) -> REVIEW -> BATCH 2 (10) -> REVIEW -> continue while added value remains high.

## Batch gate

Before a locality is activated:
1. local/community willingness or at least no evidence of conflict with the concept;
2. carrying-capacity and ecological sensitivity screen;
3. cultural/provenance screen;
4. evidence of multiple real local capacities, not one attraction only;
5. field-team feasibility;
6. guide adds something beyond existing tourism pages;
7. local economic benefit can plausibly be retained;
8. one reason to visit and one reason to protect/limit visibility must both be identifiable.

## Field model

For each locality, send a small collaborative field team where practical:
- local video/podcast/documentary creator;
- local cultural/history person;
- independent field observer;
- adaptive specialist where needed (aesthetician, psychologist/wellbeing, craft/design, ecology, food, youth, mobility, etc.).

They travel together but maintain distinct observation responsibilities and short individual field notes.

## Deliverable per locality

A living place card should answer:
- What gives people joy, fascination, learning, beauty, social life or rest here?
- What is locally made well?
- What can visitors participate in rather than merely consume?
- What history/context must be understood?
- What connects this place to nearby places?
- What local economic loop could be strengthened?
- What should not be intensified or widely promoted?
- How do residents themselves judge the proposed visibility?

## Government presentation

Present batches to Argentine authorities as a capacity-discovery and regional-distribution pilot, not as a campaign to maximise visitor volume.

Core political proposition:

'Rather than concentrating attention on a small number of destinations, Mi Argentina can make many smaller localities visible in balanced batches, helping domestic travellers distribute time and spending while allowing each place to define what it wants to share, what it wants to protect, and which local capacities it wants to strengthen.'

## Measurement

Measure more than visitor numbers:
- local share of visitor spending;
- length and seasonality of stays;
- number/diversity of local producers and cultural actors discovered;
- participation in culture/learning/social activities;
- resident satisfaction;
- ecological/carrying-capacity pressure;
- repeat travel and cross-town routes;
- whether attention is distributed across the batch rather than concentrating on one locality.

## Expansion

Do not predetermine a final number of towns. Use batches of 10 while:
- field quality remains high;
- local participation remains manageable;
- ecological safeguards remain credible;
- the guide continues to reveal material added value.

Pause or change batch size if a batch creates excessive burden, weakens editorial depth or produces attention concentration.
