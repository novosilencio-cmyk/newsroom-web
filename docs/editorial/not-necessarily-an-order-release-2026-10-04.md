## Six-stage language review

### 1. Helhetsinntrykk

The essay has a clear movement: public AI dispute → commercial/attention question → resistance to an easy judgment → human automatic patterns → bodily pause → the author's own interests → quiet observation → afterword returning explicitly to the person who judges consciousness. The final form remains essayistic rather than converting the piece into a verdict on named executives.

### 2. Styrker

- The text makes its own interest visible: the author states that with funding he would develop an AI company himself.
- The body section turns an abstract consciousness dispute into an observable human event without presenting a physiological theory as fact.
- “Program” and “prompt injection” remain metaphors, with explicit resistance to the idea that humans are merely computers.
- The forest ending demonstrates restraint rather than forcing nature into a symbolic conclusion.
- The afterword restores the originating question: how aware is the person making a judgment of consciousness?

### 3. Stilprofil

Quiet, reflective and concrete. Short paragraphs create space, but factual passages remain explicit about actor, source and evidential limits. The text asks rather than diagnoses. The sharpest question is placed in an afterword so the main essay can keep its open movement.

### 4. Konkrete svakheter checked

- The original draft moved too quickly from the company dispute to the author/reader and under-expressed the originating question about the evaluators themselves. The afterword repairs this without asserting an inner state for named people.
- Commercial interest could have been mistaken for proof of bad faith. The text explicitly states that commercial interest is not a counterargument and that publicity value is not evidence of a staged campaign.
- “Consciousness” can refer both to subjective experience and to awareness of one's own patterns. The afterword makes the distinction explicit by saying it is not asking whether the executives are awake or have an inner life.
- Translation risk is highest around this distinction. Each language version therefore uses wording that distinguishes machine consciousness from awareness/self-observation as far as the language naturally permits.

### 5. Forbedringsgrep applied

- Retain the main essay unchanged in its open rhythm after the reader review.
- Add the separate afterword rather than inserting a hard accusation into the body.
- Preserve the author's own commercial aspiration and self-application of the same question.
- Keep the source note visible and state the limits of the Morgenbladet access.
- Publish a correction invitation on every language version.
- Make Norwegian explicitly the original language and disclose AI-supported research, drafting, editing and translation.

### 6. Revidert eksempelversjon

The final Norwegian page and the nine translated pages are the revised example versions. No further wording change is required for publication within the stated source and translation limits.

## Reader journey

A reader should be able to identify:

1. the real public disagreement that triggered the essay;
2. the difference between attention/commercial positioning and proof of manipulation;
3. the shift from machine consciousness to human interpretation;
4. the author's own implication in the same field and incentives;
5. the essay's refusal to make a final claim about machine subjective experience;
6. the afterword's unresolved question about the person making the judgment.

The language picker keeps all ten versions on one story spine. The sources and AI/translation disclosure are available without interrupting the essay's main rhythm.

## Semantic control

The release preserves these distinctions across versions:

- company position ≠ independent fact;
- intended model behaviour ≠ observed model behaviour;
- functional capacity ≠ subjective experience;
- uncertainty about consciousness ≠ proof of consciousness;
- training or instruction to deny consciousness ≠ proof of absence;
- commercial interest ≠ invalid argument;
- publicity effect ≠ staged campaign;
- learned human interpretation ≠ the whole person;
- self-awareness of interpretation ≠ proof about another being's consciousness;
- authorial ambition ≠ evidence for or against the essay's claims.

## Translation review and limits
