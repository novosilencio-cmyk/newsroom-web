Before, then, if: 25 public experiments worth watching

A few hours of childcare, a restored wetland, a reliable water supply: public initiatives become interesting when we can see what they might change in daily life. These 25 cases offer concrete starting points for following that work.

Originator, editorial direction & final publication responsibility: Bjørn Moe Aldema · Writing and editorial development: Experimental Newsroom human–AI collaboration · AI editorial roles: Verana, Ariadne, Ingrid, Nova, Karl and Eva · Published by Experimental Newsroom, 31 August 2026 · Updated 2 September 2026

Sources were checked against official government or ministry material available on 31 August 2026. This is a test edition, not an endorsement.

How to read the cases: Before describes the problem or starting point. Then identifies the public action. When points to the stage at which its value can be examined. If sets out a possibility that still needs evidence. These headings guide the reading; they do not mean that every case has reached the same stage. Some results may take seasons or years to become visible. A story may begin with a result and look back to an earlier decision; that narrative order must not obscure when events happened or when evidence became available. Periods in which nothing decisive has happened can remain uncertain, without invented drama.

The publishing desk selected these cases for the test edition because each had an official source, a defined action and something observable to follow. Selection recognises their reporting value. Whether a measure works, for whom and for how long remains to be established.

Taiwan — three invitations to build capacity

TW-01 · Community net zero

What if a climate plan begins with a community rather than a machine?

Before. A national climate target can feel distant from local decisions about energy, travel and consumption.

Then. Taiwan's Climate Change Administration invited 20 communities to plan net-zero routes with technical partners. Options include resilient energy, verified emissions reductions and local circular economies.

When. After selection, the plan must become decisions the community can carry out.

If. Technical support could strengthen local decision-making. Following who can enter the pilot, and whose choices shape it, would show whether that happens.

Source: Taiwan Climate Change Administration.

TW-02 · Circular resources

A grant programme asks what happens after “waste” stops being the end of the story

Before. Packaging, electronics and building materials pass through different industries and waste systems. Keeping them useful requires connections between those systems.

Then. Taiwan opened a 2027 programme for research, demonstration and commercialisation in six resource-circulation fields, including biomass, plastics, packaging, construction and energy equipment.

When. Funded projects need to identify material that can be recovered, someone who can use it and a viable route between them.

If. Connecting design, collection and demand could make reuse dependable beyond a demonstration. That practical continuity is what to follow.

Source: Taiwan Resource Circulation Administration.

TW-03 · Technology and daily life

Taiwan asks chip researchers to imagine the life around the chip

Before. Discussions of chips often begin with performance, production capacity and strategic supply.

Then. Taiwan's science council invited humanities, design, engineering, art and social research into a programme on emerging technology and everyday behaviour, with scenarios and demonstrators among its outputs.

When. Before a design is fixed, possible uses can still challenge its assumptions.

If. Evidence about people's lives could change technical priorities. That is the opportunity to investigate, rather than an effect already shown.

Source: Taiwan National Science and Technology Council.

Denmark — changing land, changing care

DK-01 · Green Tripartite

The Danish map is being asked to do several jobs at once

Before. Farming, water quality, forest and habitat needs meet on the same land, often under different policies.

Then. Denmark's Green Tripartite brings wetlands, lowland conversion, less intensive land use and forest planting into local plans, using voluntary arrangements with landowners.

When. Between a mapped proposal and a changed field come agreements, compensation and checks of how water moves through the land.

If. Workable agreements could join ecological benefits across a landscape. The result depends on both the owners' participation and what the changes achieve.

Source: Danish Agency for Green Land Conversion and Aquatic Environment.

DK-02 · Elder care

Can fewer handovers create more care?

Before. An older person receiving care may meet many workers, and frequent changes of staff can make continuity harder to maintain.

Then. Denmark's reform and 2026 support encourage fixed multidisciplinary teams, self-determination and leadership closer to daily care. A grant pool supports local trials.

When. Follow an ordinary week: who visits, how well they know the person and what staff can decide for themselves.

If. Stable relationships and professional discretion could support high-quality care. Earlier evaluations indicate promise alongside work still to be done.

Source: Danish Health Authority / Knowledge Centre for Elder Care.

Sweden — money that asks for measurable change

SE-01 · Klimatklivet

A climate grant with a simple provocation: show the avoided emissions

Before. An organisation may know which equipment to replace but struggle to finance the change.

Then. Sweden's Klimatklivet accepted applications from 24 August to 4 September 2026 for local and regional investments that cut greenhouse-gas emissions.

When. Once equipment is operating, measured emissions can be compared with the estimate used in the application.

If. Sound selection and workable finance could turn grants into lasting reductions across many sites. Operation supplies the evidence.

Source: Swedish Environmental Protection Agency.

SE-02 · Meadows and pastures

Restoration begins with work that looks small on a national map

Before. A meadow can lose species through gradual changes in mowing, grazing or management.

Then. Sweden added a 2026 LONA funding stream for valuable grasslands. Municipalities apply, while local actors can initiate and run restoration projects.

When. The work needs to be followed over seasons, through vegetation, management and the presence or absence of species.

If. Local stewardship could accumulate benefits across connected habitats. Isolated improvements and wider ecological recovery need to be distinguished.

Source: Swedish Environmental Protection Agency.

SE-03 · Wetlands

A wetland is both an intervention and a period of waiting

Before. Drainage can change a landscape's ability to retain water and support habitats.

Then. LONA offers municipal support for new or restored wetlands, with associations and other local actors able to carry out projects. Applications for 2027 projects close on 1 December 2026.

When. Rain, drought and breeding seasons will help reveal the effects, sometimes over several years.

If. Local initiative backed by funding could restore several functions at once. Water retention, habitat and other benefits each require evidence.

Source: Swedish Environmental Protection Agency.

Japan — five ways to move from pressure to experiment

JP-01 · Smart agriculture

The robot is not the story unless the farm changes

Before. Labour shortages, ageing and climate pressures make recurring farm tasks harder to sustain.

Then. Japan's agriculture ministry supports smart-agriculture adoption and services that can reach more than one farm.

When. The test comes with maintenance, training and fieldwork, including whether a smaller farm can obtain help when needed.

If. Shared services could give more farms access to useful automation without each buying and maintaining the whole system.

Source: Japan Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

JP-02 · Universal flexible daycare

A few hours of childcare can be a small policy with a very large question

Before. Families caring for young children outside regular nursery arrangements may still need respite and contact.

Then. Japan's nationwide 2026 flexible-daycare benefit covers eligible children under three regardless of parental employment.

When. Follow the application into an available booking, prepared staff and a child settling into the visit.

If. A trusted, usable service could widen family support. Effects on isolation, employment or fertility need their own evidence.

Source: Japan Children and Families Agency.

JP-03 · Plastic circularity

Eight pilots begin where the recycling symbol usually ends

Before. Collected plastic still needs processing that produces material someone will buy.

Then. Japan selected eight 2026 implementation projects, including AI-assisted collection, construction-site separation, reuse and biomass-plastic models.

When. Quality, processing cost and repeat demand will determine whether recovered material stays useful.

If. Designing collection and sorting alongside the eventual use could help explain which recycling arrangements can endure.

Source: Japan Ministry of the Environment.

JP-04 · Municipal circular economy

Before funding a circular solution, Japan asks some places to diagnose the system

Before. A city may have recoverable resources but lack the businesses, relationships or knowledge to use them locally.

Then. Japan offered support for municipal diagnosis and planning of circular economies, together with business demonstrations and expert assistance.

When. Follow the assessment into a chosen resource flow and the organisations expected to act.

If. A grounded diagnosis could direct effort towards local opportunities that a generic circular-economy plan would miss.

Source: Japan Ministry of the Environment.

JP-05 · Decarbonisation leading regions

Japan is trying to make carbon reduction look like a place people would choose to live

Before. Climate goals can seem detached from homes, travel and energy bills.

Then. Japan's decarbonisation leading regions programme supports deep reductions in household and commercial electricity emissions by 2030, alongside local attractiveness and quality of life.

When. Plans meet the conditions of local grids, buildings, budgets and residents' choices.

If. Linking emissions cuts to useful local changes could make climate action part of everyday development. Each place needs to demonstrate that connection.

Source: Japan Ministry of the Environment.

Hong Kong — industry as an experiment in capability

HK-01 · New industrialisation

The factory returns as a policy instrument

Before. Strong research and service sectors do not automatically create a local manufacturing base.

Then. Hong Kong's New Industrialisation Acceleration Scheme supports advanced production facilities. Its threshold fell in late 2025, technical-staff support was added and 2026 approvals include semiconductor projects. Manufacturing+ supports existing production lines separately.

When. Look beyond delivered equipment to the skills, suppliers and working production that develop around it.

If. Public co-investment could create capabilities useful beyond the supported factory. Those wider effects need to be traced.

Source: Hong Kong Innovation and Technology Commission; 2026–27 Budget.

China — from “waste-free city” pilots to a national systems question

CN-01 · Waste-free cities

What changes when a pilot stops being a pilot?

Before. China's waste-free-city work began through pilots and demonstrations in selected places.

Then. The 2026 planning cycle calls for wider and more coordinated implementation, using a waste-free index to connect parts of the system.

When. Expansion brings the approach into places with different industries, staff capacity and data quality.

If. Consistent measurement and local accountability could support that expansion. National totals must still leave room to see weak local results.

Source: Ministry of Ecology and Environment of the People’s Republic of China.

Brazil — infrastructure, family farming and carbon in the soil

BR-01 · Novo PAC 2026

A government selection list becomes real only when water moves, sewage leaves and streets survive rain

Before. Poor infrastructure is experienced through unreliable water, flooding, unsafe slopes and difficult journeys.

Then. Brazil's Cities Ministry published 2026 Novo PAC selections for water, sanitation, drainage, mobility, waste and favela upgrading.

When. Selected projects must pass through purchasing, engineering and construction into maintained services.

If. Completed work in places of high need could make daily life safer and easier. Selection alone leaves that outcome ahead.

Source: Brazil Ministry of Cities.

BR-02 · Family farming

Credit matters differently when the state is also a buyer

Before. Small farms need dependable buyers as well as credit, advice and protection against risk.

Then. Brazil's 2026/27 plan combines Pronaf credit, insurance, extension and purchasing instruments. School-food and institutional purchasing rules create separate routes to public demand.

When. Follow how financing, planting decisions and purchases fit together in a particular place.

If. A more dependable market could help farms invest and diversify. Local records and producers' experience must show whether the instruments reinforce one another.

Source: Brazil Ministry of Agrarian Development and Family Agriculture; National Education Development Fund.

BR-03 · ABC+

Low-carbon agriculture becomes more interesting when the monitoring catches up

Before. Promoting agroforestry, pasture recovery, bioinputs and integrated farming is easier than measuring their uptake.

Then. Brazil's ABC+ combines state governance with national dashboards for ten sustainable practices, alongside revisions to governance and baselines.

When. Compare reported adoption with verifiable changes in land use and practice.

If. Reliable monitoring could help redirect support towards what works, giving agricultural climate policy a way to learn.

Source: Brazil Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock.

Argentina — literacy and the workshop floor

AR-01 · National Literacy Plan

Twenty-four jurisdictions, one basic promise: children should be able to read what comes next

Before. Argentina's official diagnosis identifies substantial difficulty with minimum reading comprehension among third-grade pupils.

Then. The federal government and all 24 jurisdictions have literacy plans, with teacher development, materials, monitoring and 2026 coordination through the Federal Education Council.

When. Follow the plans into classroom time, teaching, books and measures of learning.

If. Comparable evidence alongside local delivery could help the programme learn across jurisdictions and sustain useful teaching practices.

Source: Argentina Ministry of Human Capital / Federal Education Council.

AR-02 · KIT 4.0

Digital transformation, reduced to a smaller question: what changes on Monday morning?

Before. A small business needs a workable first purchase, while Industry 4.0 can describe an overwhelming range of choices.

Then. Argentina created KIT 4.0 in 2026 to co-finance standardised digital services and implementations for small and medium-sized firms.

When. The useful measures are operational: downtime, quality, information flow, energy and what staff can do.

If. Standard packages could ease a first step, provided they fit the business and leave it able to change course later.

Source: Argentina Ministry of Economy.

A deliberate turn in the map

Five more countries — chosen for what they let us test

The next five cases broaden the comparison. South Korea tests automation in open fields; Singapore combines ways of protecting food supplies; Chile connects industrial plans with local conditions; Rwanda links finance to farming; India follows water infrastructure into maintenance.

Each asks what people and institutions must be able to do after a programme is launched.

South Korea — automation meets the open field

KR-01 · Open-field smart agriculture

Five regions become a test of whether automation can work outside the greenhouse

Before. Weather, soil and large areas make open-field automation different from work in controlled growing environments.

Then. South Korea selected five smart-agriculture zones for 2026, each roughly 500 hectares. They combine autonomous equipment, water and fertiliser systems, pest monitoring, communications and farmer training.

When. A growing season will show how equipment, agronomy and people work together under changing conditions.

If. Reduced labour pressure would be more valuable if farmers also gained the knowledge and control needed to adapt the system.

Source: Republic of Korea Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs.

Singapore — resilience built from more than one answer

SG-01 · Food Story 2

Food resilience begins with admitting that no single solution can carry the load

Before. Singapore imports more than 90 per cent of its food, exposing supplies to disruption elsewhere.

Then. Food Story 2 combines diversified imports, international partnerships, stockpiles and local production.

When. A disrupted route tests whether the other arrangements can cover the shortfall.

If. The approaches could complement one another under stress. How far they can compensate, and at what cost, determines the practical resilience.

Source: Singapore Ministry of Sustainability and the Environment.

Chile — when industrial ambition meets territory

CL-01 · Green hydrogen

After the ambition comes the harder chapter: where will a new industry actually fit?

Before. Chile's first green-hydrogen strategy set ambitious goals based on renewable-energy potential.

Then. After consultation, the Energy Ministry updated the strategy for 2026–2030 with priorities, milestones and critical issues for the next phase.

When. Projects bring those ambitions into contact with water, infrastructure, local economies and environmental limits.

If. Competitive production could bring local benefits if project choices respond to the places and people affected. Acceptance cannot be inferred from national potential.

Source: Chile Ministry of Energy.

Rwanda — the distance between finance and the farm

RW-01 · Climate-smart agriculture

Forty million euros is not yet a harvest

Before. Climate shocks and limited investment constrain the choices available to smallholders.

Then. Rwanda and the EU signed the five-year, €40 million HANGARIBIHE programme in August 2026, combining budget support, technical help, studies and capacity development for inclusive, climate-smart agriculture.

When. Follow national support into advice, finance and technology that farmers can actually access.

If. Adaptable help at farm level could expand people's choices. Funding that remains at institutional level would leave that benefit unproven.

Source: Government of Rwanda / Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning.

India — infrastructure after the ribbon is cut

IN-01 · Jal Jeevan Mission

A tap is an endpoint only if the source and the village system keep working

Before. Reliable drinking water requires a source, sound water quality and people able to maintain the system.

Then. India's Jal Jeevan Mission links household taps with source sustainability, rainwater harvesting, grey-water management and village planning and maintenance.

When. Dry periods, repairs and quality problems test what happens after installation.

If. Capable local committees could keep connections useful over time. Recording taps alongside reliable service would show how far that happens.

Source: India Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation, Ministry of Jal Shakti.

What this field lab is testing

Together, the cases give us 25 starting points for reporting. Each identifies an action and a later observation that could change our assessment. They are neither a government ranking nor a list of proven solutions.

Some will unfold through decisions and deliveries; others need seasons or years. We will follow the evidence at that pace. The interest lies in what changes for people and places, including the periods when a result remains uncertain.

Editorial boundary: Experimental Newsroom may identify where a measure appears to need capability, collaboration or support. That observation is not an offer of support and does not imply contact, funding, partnership or mandate. Any such question belongs to a separate Samfunnskapasitet stewardship process.
