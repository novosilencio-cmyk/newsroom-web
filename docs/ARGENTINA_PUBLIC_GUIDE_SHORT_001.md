# Discover Argentina — and what Argentines make possible

A physical and digital national guide designed to help people discover more of their own country: its places, people, knowledge, food, craft, art, nature and opportunities to learn and participate.

## The invitation

See what we have.
Discover where it is.
Meet people who know and make things.
Taste, listen, learn and explore.
Travel further in your own country.
Support worthwhile local production.
Create something yourself.
Help build what comes next.

## What the guide shows

For towns, villages and regions:

- regional food, farms, markets, cafés and restaurants;
- furniture, craft, workshops, repair and local manufacturing;
- music, dance, theatre, literature, art and festivals;
- universities, libraries, public lectures, workshops and research;
- local and Indigenous histories represented with proper participation and provenance;
- mountains, forests, steppe, wetlands, coast, rivers, thermal springs and dark skies;
- skiing, walking, cycling, riding, paddling and accessible family activities;
- locally rooted places to stay;
- seasonal calendars and events;
- train, bus, bicycle and electric-mobility routes;
- worthwhile local goods to take home;
- locally identified needs and opportunities;
- guidance for visiting without damaging what makes a place valuable.

## Two connected objects

**The booklet:** beautiful, useful, durable and physically distributed.

**The living atlas:** maps, current schedules, deeper stories, accessibility information, routes and community corrections reached through QR codes and short links.

## Distribution is part of the design

A PDF on a government website is not enough.

Put the booklet where people already are: schools, universities, libraries, stations, municipal and provincial offices, cultural centres, museums, tourism offices, community organisations, bookstores, selected cafés and national/regional events.

Reach provinces and smaller communities, not only Buenos Aires.

## First pilot

Create full profiles for 5–10 very different Argentine places. Compare them with existing guides. Continue only if the new guide makes valuable culture, learning, local production or connections materially easier to discover.
