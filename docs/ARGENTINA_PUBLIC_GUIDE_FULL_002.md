# Argentina — public discovery guide full version 002

Status: INTERNAL PRODUCT DRAFT / NOT PUBLISHED / NOT SENT
Intended future owner if adopted: Argentine public authorities or a designated public-purpose partner.

## Working civic motif

# Mi Argentina

A national discovery guide that helps people notice, enjoy, value, care for and cultivate what is already worthwhile in Argentina.

Not a propaganda object. Not a tourism catalogue. Not a ranking machine.

It should help people discover more of their own country: its people, places, knowledge, food, craft, music, dance, celebration, science, nature, production, learning and opportunities to participate.

## Human aim

The guide should make it easier to encounter things that can support:

- health and restoration;
- joy and social connection;
- fascination and curiosity;
- inspiration and learning;
- pride grounded in real things worth valuing;
- creativity and initiative;
- belonging without exclusion;
- intergenerational discovery;
- meaningful local and national participation.

Wellbeing is not treated as a productivity instrument. It matters in itself and may also affect which human capacities are accessible under current conditions.

## Invitation

See what we have.
Discover where it is.
Meet people who know, make, teach, perform and care for things.
Eat, drink, listen, dance, celebrate, learn, rest and explore.
Travel further in your own country.
Support worthwhile local and national production.
Try something new.
Create something yourself.
Care for what is worth keeping.
Help cultivate what comes next.

## Realistic social life

The guide should represent Argentine life as lived rather than dividing people into narrow age stereotypes.

Children may be delighted by animals, trains, water, stories, food, music, making and discovery.

Young people may value friendship, celebration, concerts, festivals, dancing, nightlife, sport, nature, technology, learning, travel and places to meet.

Adults and families may want many of the same things alongside food, culture, practical learning, rest, work opportunities and shared experiences.

Older and mature people may also seek dancing, festivals, friendship, gastronomy, travel, sport, adventure, learning, volunteering, nightlife, art and new skills — not only quiet heritage activities.

The interface should therefore allow discovery by interest, mood, accessibility need, time and place, not only by age.

## Food, drink and sociability

Argentina's food and drink cultures should be represented as part of living social culture: meals, cafés, markets, peñas, restaurants, regional products, wine culture, juices and non-alcoholic drinks, seasonal foods and celebrations.

Alcohol may be included where it is genuinely part of adult regional culture, but it should never be presented as required for belonging, celebration or identity. Non-alcoholic routes and family/youth alternatives should be equally discoverable.

## Celebration, dance and festivals

Popular festivals, music, dance, milongas, peñas, theatre, sport, fairs and communal celebrations belong near the centre of the guide rather than being decorative extras.

Argentina's own cultural sources describe festivals as expressions of cultural heritage that can strengthen federal identity, local management, regional economies and cultural tourism. Existing national data report thousands of popular festivals and festivals across the country.

The guide should show not only what event exists, but:

- why the celebration matters locally;
- who carries the tradition;
- what people can experience or learn;
- what food, music, dance, craft and history surround it;
- how to participate respectfully;
- how to avoid excessive pressure on fragile communities or places.

## Tango and living heritage

Tango should be represented as a living, participatory culture — music, lyrics, social dance, milongas, classes, performance, history and contemporary practice — rather than a static national symbol.

The same principle applies to other living traditions across Argentina: heritage is something people continue to practise, teach, celebrate and transform.

## Design principle — gratitude as observation

Begin by noticing what is already valuable: a workshop, a landscape, a library, a regional dish, a railway, a dance tradition, a scientific institution, a thermal spring, a small producer, a neighbourhood club, a festival, a public lecture or a place where people teach one another.

Then ask:

- What makes this valuable?
- Which human need or joy does it serve?
- Who sustains it?
- How can people discover it without degrading it?
- Can visibility strengthen the local capacity that carries it?
- What should be protected from over-commercialisation?

## The two connected objects

### Physical guide

A beautiful, practical object people can hold, keep, annotate, lend and take on a journey.

Possible editions:

- national discovery edition;
- provincial/regional inserts;
- children/family discovery edition;
- youth edition;
- annual or seasonal editions;
- thematic editions: food, dance, music, science, craft, rail, thermal landscapes, nature, architecture, sport, learning, festivals.

### Living digital atlas

Maps, current schedules, events, transport, accessibility, local producers, learning opportunities, deeper stories and community corrections.

The digital layer should support discovery modes such as:

- I want to celebrate;
- I want to dance;
- I want to learn;
- I want to taste something;
- I want to make something;
- I want to be outdoors;
- I want to rest;
- I want to meet people;
- I want to take children somewhere fascinating;
- I want an accessible experience;
- I want something surprising near me.

## Living place portrait

Each town, neighbourhood, village or region can combine:

Food and raw materials; cafés, restaurants and markets; wine and non-alcoholic regional drinks; craft and production; repair; art; dance; live music; theatre; cinema; literature; nightlife and social venues; festivals; learning and research; local history; Indigenous histories with participation and provenance; nature and landscape; sport and outdoor activity; thermal and restorative experiences; locally rooted accommodation; calendars; transport and onward routes; worthwhile local goods; locally identified needs and opportunities; ecological and cultural limits.

## Discovery by life and interest, not stereotype

Every place card can carry tags across several dimensions:

- social / quiet;
- playful / contemplative;
- family / adult / all-ages;
- free / low-cost / premium;
- indoor / outdoor;
- learning / celebration / restoration / adventure / making;
- accessible features;
- season and time of day;
- public transport / walking / cycling / EV access.

No one should be forced into an age-defined cultural lane.

## Distribution and attention are part of the product

A guide that exists only as a PDF has failed this objective.

It should be present where people already spend time, attention and money:

- schools and universities;
- libraries;
- railway and bus stations;
- municipal and provincial offices;
- cultural centres and museums;
- tourism offices;
- community organisations;
- shopping centres and commercial streets;
- markets and fairs;
- workplaces where appropriate;
- sports venues;
- festivals and major public events;
- bookstores, cafés and locally rooted businesses;
- selected public-service locations.

Shopping-centre and high-traffic communication should not shame people for buying things. Its role is to make worthwhile Argentine alternatives visible at the moment attention and purchasing decisions already exist.

Candidate prompts:

- Discover something Argentine nearby.
- What is made in your region?
- What could you experience this weekend that you did not know existed?
- Meet the people who make it.

Commercial payment must not buy editorial inclusion.

## Pride without superiority

The guide may intentionally support affection and pride in Argentina, but pride should have observable objects: people, landscapes, institutions, traditions, inventions, food, art, knowledge, work, public achievements and communities worth appreciating.

The underlying movement is:

notice -> discover -> value -> care for -> cultivate and tend.

Not: Argentina is better than other countries.

## Community contribution

local proposal -> provenance check -> editorial verification -> Argentina-context review -> inclusion candidate -> publication/update.

Communities should be able to correct errors, nominate overlooked practices and places, and signal when visibility is creating harmful pressure.

## Economic and societal hypothesis

visibility -> curiosity -> social/cultural participation and domestic discovery -> local/national spending and learning -> stronger producers, institutions and communities -> more reasons and capacity to discover, create and participate.

This is a hypothesis to measure, not a promised result.

Possible measures include local value retained, cultural participation, discovery of low-visibility producers/institutions, repeat domestic travel, public-transport use, local satisfaction, youth/family participation, intergenerational participation, learning activity and ecological pressure.

## Editorial safeguards

- invitation, not propaganda;
- joy is not a productivity tool;
- no age stereotypes;
- alcohol is optional adult culture, never a belonging requirement;
- communities are not tourism products;
- payment does not determine inclusion;
- local/traditional is not automatically better;
- new/popular is not automatically better;
- Indigenous/local knowledge requires provenance and participation;
- preserve what makes a place valuable rather than maximise visitor volume;
- psychological motivation should support autonomy, not manipulation;
- remove or revise recommendations when evidence shows harm.

## Pilot

Select 5–10 culturally, economically and geographically different places and at least two different urban contexts. Include a real mix of food, celebration, learning, production, nature and social life.

For each:

1. build the living place portrait;
2. test language with Argentina-specific editorial review;
3. involve local sources and institutions;
4. map events, social life and intergenerational options as well as conventional attractions;
5. compare against existing Argentine public guides and cultural maps;
6. test whether the prototype reveals materially more useful capacity;
7. measure local burden, accessibility and ecological/cultural pressure;
8. stop if added value is weak.
