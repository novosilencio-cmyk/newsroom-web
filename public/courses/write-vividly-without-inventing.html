Level 1 · factual writing

Write vividly without inventing

Make nonfiction alive without inventing scenes, motives, feelings or certainty.

Originator & idea-maker: Mr.BM.Benlolo · Human adaptation and editorial development: Experimental Newsroom human–AI collaboration · Published: 2 September 2026

You need

One bounded source set and somewhere to write.

You will make

A 250–450 word text, truth map, movement and revision note.

Time

About 100 minutes, plus optional reader feedback.

Before you register

What the course contains

  1. Anchor: give the reader somewhere documented to stand.
  2. Movement: anchor → tension → development → turn → return.
  3. Truth map: distinguish documented, attributed, inferred, reconstructed and unknown.
  4. Detail and explanation: create presence without making scenes.
  5. Controlled example: compare thin, over-told and supportable versions.
  6. Three revision passes: truth, movement and human consequence.
  7. Final work: produce, test and explain a bounded factual text.

TRY

Public sample exercise

Make a five-line movement

Choose one source-supported subject. Write only one line for each stage:

  1. Anchor: a concrete starting point.
  2. Tension: something unresolved, difficult or changing.
  3. Development: a source or explanation makes the picture richer.
  4. Turn: a limitation, contrast or fact changes the understanding.
  5. Return: the opening is seen again with more meaning.

Boundary check

Point to support for every line. If the source cannot carry a complete movement, deliver an honest incomplete one. Do not fill the gap with atmosphere or certainty.

Method and standard

Practice, attempted disproof, independent reading

The course asks you to make observable work, write a deliberately invalid negative control, and let another reader assess the frozen text without first hearing your intention. AI may help with structure and revision, but a human owns source selection, verification, privacy, harm, publication and corrections.
