# Argentina — public discovery guide full version 001

Status: INTERNAL PRODUCT DRAFT / NOT PUBLISHED / NOT SENT
Intended future owner if adopted: Argentine public authorities or a designated public-purpose partner.

## Purpose

Create a national discovery guide that helps people living in Argentina see more clearly what their country already contains and what they can experience, learn from, support and help develop.

The guide is not a tourism catalogue and not state propaganda. It should connect culture, knowledge, nature, local production and domestic travel in a form that increases curiosity and useful participation while respecting ecological and cultural limits.

## Core invitation

See what we have.
Discover where it is.
Meet the people who know and make things.
Learn something.
Taste and experience something.
Travel further in your own country.
Buy and use local when it is genuinely worthwhile.
Create something yourself.
Help build the country forward.

## Design principle — gratitude as observation

Begin by noticing what is already valuable: a craft, a landscape, a public library, a regional dish, a workshop, a railway, a dance tradition, a scientific institution, a thermal spring, a small manufacturer or a place where people teach one another.

Then ask what makes it valuable, how that function can be preserved, and whether greater visibility can improve life without degrading the source of the value.

The guide should celebrate useful modern achievements as well as inherited traditions. It should not romanticise the past or treat novelty as progress by definition.

## The physical guide

A well-made printed object people can hold, keep, annotate, lend and take on a journey.

Possible editions:

- national discovery edition;
- province/region inserts;
- youth/school edition;
- annual or seasonal edition;
- thematic editions: food, craft, science, music, rail journeys, thermal landscapes, nature, architecture, learning.

## The living digital atlas

The printed guide links through QR codes, short links and place identifiers to a continuously maintained digital layer with:

- maps and routes;
- current opening times and event calendars;
- public transport and accessibility;
- deeper cultural and historical material;
- local producers and learning opportunities;
- ecological/seasonal guidance;
- corrections and locally proposed additions.

The printed object provides orientation and discovery. The digital layer provides freshness and depth.

## Living place portrait

Each included town, village or region can be represented through the following dimensions.

### Food and raw materials
Regional dishes, bakeries, cafés, restaurants, farms, markets, wine, cheese, fruit, vegetables, distinctive ingredients and seasonal traditions.

### Craft, repair and production
Furniture, woodworking, ceramics, textiles, leather, metal, instrument making, repair workshops, small manufacturers and regional industrial specialities.

### Arts and culture
Music, dance, theatre, cinema, literature, storytelling, visual arts, galleries, festivals, architecture, cultural houses and living traditions.

### Learning and knowledge
Universities, colleges, vocational schools, libraries, public lectures, workshops, short courses, research centres, local experts and knowledge traditions.

### History and people
Local history, migration histories, notable local figures, oral heritage and Indigenous histories where relevant, represented with participation, provenance and cultural respect.

### Nature and landscape
Mountains, forests, steppe, wetlands, coast, lakes, rivers, desert, thermal springs, geology, wildlife and dark-sky locations.

### Activity
Skiing, hiking, cycling, riding, paddling, climbing, bathing, thermal experiences and accessible family activities where appropriate.

### Stay
Small hotels, guesthouses, rural stays and architecturally or culturally distinctive accommodation rooted in the locality.

### Calendar
Festivals, performances, lectures, markets, harvests, food seasons, exhibitions, sport and community events.

### How to arrive and continue
Train, bus, bicycle, realistic walking links, electric-vehicle corridors, charging, scenic routes and the next worthwhile nearby place.

### What to bring home
Durable local goods, foods, books, art, craft and repairable locally made products where genuinely worthwhile.

### What the place says it needs
Where communities and authorities choose to publish it: skills gaps, restoration needs, cultural preservation, infrastructure opportunities, training, business opportunities and civic initiatives.

### Visit respectfully
Ecological limits, seasonal carrying capacity, protected areas, local cultural guidance, accessibility, safety and explicit protection against turning fragile places into mass destinations.

## A discovery network, not a ranking machine

The system should help people answer questions such as:

- What is worth travelling for?
- What could I learn here?
- Who makes something exceptional here?
- What happens here in another season?
- What nearby place complements this one?
- Can I arrive without a private fossil-fuel car?
- What can I support locally without degrading the place?

Popularity alone should not determine visibility. Lesser-known places need a path into the system.

## Distribution — getting it into people's hands

Distribution is a core product function.

A guide that exists only as a downloadable PDF has failed this objective.

Potential distribution network:

- schools and universities;
- public and university libraries;
- railway and bus stations;
- municipal and provincial offices;
- cultural centres and museums;
- tourism offices;
- community organisations;
- selected public-service locations;
- bookstores and locally rooted cafés/businesses;
- major national and regional events.

The distribution plan must explicitly cover provinces and smaller communities, not only Buenos Aires.

Possible mechanisms include free copies, low-cost durable editions, school copies, regional inserts and rotating thematic editions.

## Community feedback

The atlas should not become a purely top-down catalogue.

Suggested flow:

local proposal -> provenance check -> editorial verification -> local/context review -> inclusion candidate -> publication/update.

Communities can correct factual errors, propose overlooked people and places, and identify pressures created by excessive visitation.

## Economic hypothesis

The guide tests whether better visibility and connection can produce a constructive domestic loop:

visibility -> curiosity -> domestic travel/learning -> local spending and participation -> stronger local producers/cultural institutions -> more reasons to discover the region -> stronger regional capacity.

This must be measured rather than assumed.

Possible indicators:

- discovery of previously low-visibility producers/institutions;
- visits and repeat domestic travel;
- spending retained locally;
- participation in learning and culture;
- public-transport and low-impact mobility use;
- seasonal/geographic visitor distribution;
- local satisfaction;
- ecological pressure and carrying-capacity indicators.

## Editorial safeguards

- invitation, not propaganda;
- communities are not tourism products;
- culture is not decorative content;
- payment cannot determine editorial inclusion;
- local/traditional is not automatically better;
- new/popular is not automatically better;
- Indigenous and local knowledge requires provenance and participation;
- beauty, usefulness, learning and human experience matter alongside popularity;
- preserve what makes a place worth discovering rather than maximise visitor volume;
- remove or revise recommendations when evidence shows harm.

## Pilot architecture

Select 5–10 culturally, economically and geographically different places.

For each:

1. build the full living place portrait;
2. involve local sources and institutions;
3. map transport and neighbouring connections;
4. compare against existing Argentine national/provincial platforms;
5. identify information or capacity that existing guides miss;
6. test local burden and update requirements;
7. define ecological and cultural safeguards;
8. measure whether the prototype changes discovery or useful participation.

Pass only if the pilot demonstrates material added value such as better discovery of local cultural/productive capacity, stronger connections between places, useful learning content missing from existing guides, realistic benefit for local actors, or a scalable architecture without excessive burden.

If it adds little beyond existing services, do not scale it.
