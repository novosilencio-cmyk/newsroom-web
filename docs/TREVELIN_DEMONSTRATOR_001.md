# Trevelin — Mi Argentina demonstrator 001

Status: INTERNAL DEMONSTRATOR / NOT PUBLISHED / NOT SENT
Purpose: show Argentine public authorities how the Mi Argentina model could work in one small place before scaling in batches.

## Why Trevelin

Trevelin is suitable because existing public material already shows a dense combination of local production, tourism, culture, landscape and identity. The town and surrounding parajes connect Welsh and Mapuche-Tehuelche histories, local gastronomy, vineyards, fine fruits, textiles, tulips, hiking, snow activities, protected nature and proximity to Los Alerces.

The municipality itself currently describes a development model in which tourism can grow out of production: agricultural, viticultural and other producers add visitor-facing experiences that increase value around existing productive activity.

This makes Trevelin a useful test of a wider hypothesis:

**Can better discovery of what a place already does well increase local wellbeing and value retention without forcing the place to become a tourism product?**

## Human discovery entrances

The guide should not begin with a ranked attraction list. It should let people enter through motives:

- I want to taste something local.
- I want to learn something.
- I want to meet people and take part in social life.
- I want to see something beautiful.
- I want to be outdoors.
- I want to make or learn a craft.
- I want to find something well made locally.
- I want to show children something memorable.
- I want to rest.
- I want to be surprised.

Different ages can use the same entrances. Age is context, not a stereotype.

## Existing capacities to connect

### Food and drink
- Welsh tea traditions;
- lamb and trout;
- fine fruits and homemade preserves;
- local vineyards and wine;
- non-alcoholic food and drink experiences should be represented equally where relevant.

### Local production
- fine-fruit cultivation;
- vineyards and wineries;
- textiles and weaving;
- flowers and horticulture;
- other local agricultural and craft producers discovered through fieldwork.

### Culture and history
- Welsh migration and settlement history;
- Mapuche-Tehuelche presence and contemporary communities;
- Chilean migration and wider pluricultural local history;
- museum, mill, chapels, music, local celebrations and living traditions.

No culture should be reduced to decorative tourism content. Communities should participate in how they are represented.

### Nature and activity
- Los Alerces landscape and nearby protected areas;
- Cascadas Nant y Fall;
- Lago Rosario and Sierra Colorada;
- walking, trekking, riding and snow activities;
- seasonal flower landscapes such as the tulip fields.

### Social and festive life
Fieldwork should map music, dance, food events, festivals, sports, evening social spaces and community gatherings rather than assuming that culture is only museums and heritage sites.

## The local value loop to investigate

local producer -> refinement -> café/restaurant/hospitality -> cultural or learning experience -> local transport -> local retail/craft -> longer stay -> more local value retained -> stronger capability to maintain and improve the place.

This is a hypothesis, not a promised effect.

We should also map leakage: which visitor needs are currently met almost entirely by outside suppliers, and which of those could realistically be met locally at equal or better quality?

## Field method — Michelin-inspired, not copied

A small field team travels together.

Suggested core:
- local video/podcast producer or documentary creator;
- local cultural/historical specialist;
- independent field observer;
- aesthetic specialist when appropriate;
- local production/business contact;
- local community/Indigenous participation where relevant to the material being covered.

The team receives a bounded route and common brief, but each member also keeps an individual observation note so group consensus does not erase divergent findings.

### Example assignment

Spend 3–4 days in Trevelin and nearby parajes.

Find and document at minimum:
- one producer;
- one maker/craftsperson;
- one food or hospitality experience;
- one cultural or musical experience;
- one learning site or person;
- one nature experience;
- one social gathering/place;
- one thing children may find memorable;
- one place/product/activity that deserves more visibility;
- one fragile or saturated place that should **not** receive more promotion.

The team should ask local people: **What would you proudly show a friend? What is overlooked? What should be protected from too much attention?**

## Media output

The physical guide gives orientation.
The digital layer gives living depth.

Possible short video/podcast elements:
- 4 minutes with a local maker;
- a vineyard or fruit producer explaining the work;
- a historian walking through one local story;
- music or dance in context;
- a family or youth perspective on a favourite place;
- a nature guide explaining what visitors should protect;
- a local business showing how production and visitor activity connect.

Local creators are paid for the work, not for positive coverage.

## Aesthetic review

The aesthetic specialist asks:
- what does Trevelin actually look, sound, smell and feel like?
- which materials, colours, architecture, landscapes and rhythms are locally characteristic?
- how can the guide preserve that character rather than impose generic tourism aesthetics?
- which modern interventions improve experience and which visually or materially degrade it?

## Policy presentation

The pilot should be presented to public authorities as **local capacity development**, not a campaign for maximum visitor numbers.

Suggested policy question:

**Can Trevelin retain more value from the attention it already attracts by connecting production, culture, learning, hospitality and nature more intelligently — while protecting local life and environmental limits?**

## Measures

Do not use visitor count alone.
Track candidates such as:
- local share of visitor spending;
- length of stay;
- seasonality;
- discovery of small producers and cultural actors;
- participation in learning/cultural activities;
- resident satisfaction;
- local business usefulness;
- ecological pressure;
- traffic/congestion where relevant;
- distribution of benefits across the town and parajes;
- whether local people feel the guide represents the place truthfully.

## Batch context

Trevelin is demonstrator #1, not the sole focus.
It should be presented together with nine additional small-place candidates so attention can be distributed and the method can be compared across different cultural, ecological and economic contexts.

The first batch currently includes Trevelin, Gaiman, Villa Tulumba, Maimará, Colonia Carlos Pellegrini, Seclantás, Famatina, Saldungaray, Zenón Pereyra and El Trapiche, subject to local verification and replacement where fit is weak.

## Stop conditions

Do not scale if:
- the guide adds little beyond existing local/national services;
- residents do not want increased visibility;
- ecological or social burden exceeds likely value;
- benefits leak away without strengthening local capacity;
- field representation proves culturally shallow or extractive.
