# ARGENTINA_ACCOUNTABILITY_MAP_001

Status: `INTERNAL RESEARCH MAP / NOT PUBLIC / NOT FACT-LOCKED`
Date: 2026-08-28

## Purpose

Map responsibility before publication. The aim is not credential-based attack, but accountable journalism: identify mandate, expertise, decision ownership, implementation responsibility, evidence used and outcome verification.

## Layers to map

1. Formal mandate — law, decree, adopted budget, delegated authority.
2. Political leadership — minister, secretary, subsecretary and named decision owners.
3. Technical design — policy teams, advisers, budget offices, evaluation units, data/statistics and external expertise.
4. Implementation — agencies, universities, programmes and administrative carriers.
5. Oversight — congress, audit/evaluation bodies, public reporting and independent review.
6. Outcomes — fiscal, educational, labour-market, knowledge-economy and distributional effects.

## Professional-background rule

Publicly documented education and professional background may be examined when it helps answer a material journalistic question about role fit, expertise, institutional composition or the decision process.

Do not infer policy quality from credentials alone. Do not infer the educational level of an entire ministry from its leadership titles. Prefer institutional competence mapping: who actually supplied economic, educational, legal, statistical and implementation expertise?

## Current first-pass observations

The Ministry of Human Capital publicly lists its education leadership with formal titles including Doctor, Magister and Licenciado/a. This establishes that several senior education officials have higher-education credentials, but does not establish the education profile of the ministry's 8,000+ staff or the quality of specific policy decisions.

Carlos Torrendell, Secretary of Education, has a degree and doctorate in Education and academic experience in educational policy. Alejandro Ciro Álvarez, Subsecretary for University Policies, is publicly described as a graduate in International Relations and university teacher. The economic-policy side should be mapped separately rather than collapsed into the education ministry.

## Stronger journalistic questions

- Who framed the fiscal assumptions affecting universities and research?
- Which education specialists participated before decisions were taken?
- Were alternative adjustment paths modelled?
- What indicators distinguish efficiency gains from loss of educational capacity?
- Is there an explicit model connecting school completion, university capacity, research and the growing knowledge economy?
- Which risks were recorded internally before implementation?
- Who owns follow-up when indicators deteriorate?
- How are policy corrections triggered?

## Candidate name for the angle

`ACCOUNTABILITY_MAPPING` is clearer than personal scrutiny. In Norwegian working language: `ansvarsplassering`, `ansvarskartlegging` or `stille ansvar` as a journalistic working phrase.

## Reuse test

If this map materially improves Argentina, test the same pattern on Japan and South Korea before proposing it as a general newsroom/OØS search rule.
