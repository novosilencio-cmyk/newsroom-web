# Trevelin — government demonstrator card 001

Status: INTERNAL / NOT PUBLISHED / NOT SENT
Audience: Argentine public authorities and public-purpose partners
Purpose: make the Mi Argentina model understandable in 2–4 pages before any larger proposal.

## Page 1 — Why Trevelin

Trevelin is a useful first demonstrator because local and national public material already shows a dense combination of production, tourism, culture, history, landscape and identity.

A current municipal development line explicitly links production and tourism: local agricultural, viticultural and other producers can add visitor-facing experiences that increase value around work that already exists. The municipality has also presented FiViT as a meeting point between production, tourism, culture and community.

Trevelin is also presented municipally as pluricultural, shaped by Mapuche-Tehuelche communities, Welsh settlement history and Chilean migration. This makes it suitable for testing whether a national discovery guide can represent identity with depth rather than reducing it to decorative tourism imagery.

### Core question

**Can Trevelin retain more value from the attention it already attracts by connecting production, culture, learning, hospitality and nature more intelligently — while protecting local life and environmental limits?**

This is not a proposal to maximise visitor numbers.

## Page 2 — How people discover the place

The guide does not begin with a ranked attraction list. It begins with human motives.

### I want to taste
Welsh tea traditions, regional food, fine fruits, preserves, vineyards and local wine, alongside strong non-alcoholic options and local everyday food culture.

### I want to learn
Pluricultural history, local production, viticulture, agriculture, landscape, museums, local knowledge and contemporary community perspectives.

### I want to meet people
Producers, makers, musicians, guides, teachers, hospitality workers and community actors.

### I want to see something beautiful
Andean landscape, flower cultivation, architecture, cultivated land, rivers, forests and seasonal changes.

### I want to be outside
Walking, trekking, riding, snow activities and nearby protected landscapes, with explicit ecological limits.

### I want to find something well made
Textiles, local agricultural products, food, craft and other locally produced goods discovered and verified through fieldwork.

### I want to celebrate
Music, cultural events, food events, FiViT, social gathering places and other living forms of celebration.

### I want to show children something memorable
Production, landscape, animals, workshops, food, stories, nature and participatory experiences rather than passive consumption only.

### I want to be surprised
The field team must deliberately search beyond the already famous attractions.

## Page 3 — The local value network

The hypothesis to test is not 'more tourism'. It is better connection and more useful local value retention.

**producer -> refinement -> café/restaurant/hospitality -> cultural or learning experience -> local mobility -> local retail/craft -> longer stay -> more local value retained -> greater capability to maintain and improve the place**

### Questions for fieldwork

- Which local products already have high quality but low visibility?
- Which visitor needs are currently met mostly by outside suppliers?
- Which of those needs could realistically be met locally at equal or better quality?
- Which cultural and learning experiences encourage people to stay longer rather than simply pass through?
- Which parts of the local economy gain from current visitor flows, and which do not?
- What do residents themselves want visitors to discover?
- What should not receive more promotion because it is fragile, saturated or culturally inappropriate to expose further?

### Field method

A small field team works together for 3–4 days:

- local video/podcast or documentary creator;
- local cultural/historical specialist;
- independent field observer;
- aesthetic specialist;
- local production/business contact;
- community/Indigenous participation where relevant.

They share a route and brief, but each keeps an individual observation note.

Minimum assignment:

- one producer;
- one maker/craftsperson;
- one food/hospitality experience;
- one cultural or musical experience;
- one learning site/person;
- one nature experience;
- one social gathering place;
- one memorable child/family experience;
- one overlooked quality deserving more visibility;
- one thing that should **not** receive more promotion.

Local creators are paid for the work, not for positive coverage.

## Page 4 — What government can test without committing to a large programme

### Small pilot

1. Verify the local capacities and limits with Trevelin actors.
2. Run the 3–4 day field assignment.
3. Produce one physical place card and one digital prototype.
4. Map local value retention and leakage.
5. Ask residents and local institutions whether the representation is truthful and useful.
6. Compare with existing local/provincial/national tourism and culture services.
7. Publish nothing at scale until ecological, cultural and local-benefit controls pass.

### Measures

Do not judge success by visitor count alone.

Candidate measures:

- local share of visitor spending;
- length of stay;
- seasonal distribution;
- discovery of small producers and cultural actors;
- participation in learning and cultural activity;
- resident satisfaction;
- usefulness to local businesses and institutions;
- ecological pressure and congestion;
- distribution of benefits across Trevelin and nearby parajes;
- whether local people feel accurately represented.

### Why Trevelin is only demonstrator #1

Trevelin should be presented together with nine additional small-place candidates. The point is to distribute attention, compare the method across different regions and avoid concentrating demand on one small locality.

**Batch 1:** Trevelin, Gaiman, Villa Tulumba, Maimará, Colonia Carlos Pellegrini, Seclantás, Famatina, Saldungaray, Zenón Pereyra and El Trapiche — all subject to local verification and replacement if fit is weak.

### Decision rule

Scale only if the pilot reveals material added value that existing services do not already provide, local actors want the visibility, and benefits can be increased without unacceptable social or ecological pressure.

If not, stop or redesign.
