## External source pass

The reporting reopened the main external sources before release. The following claims are source-bound:

1. **Permaculture literature across languages.** Taylor, Clark and Malard-Adam (2025), *Agronomy for Sustainable Development*, analysed 975 publications in English, Portuguese, Spanish and French. Design, agriculture and ecology were among the strongest recurring associations, with social/community, education and sustainability also present across language sets.
   - https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13593-025-01017-2

2. **Central-European farm comparison.** Reiff et al. (2024), *Communications Earth & Environment*, compared nine permaculture sites in Germany/Luxembourg with paired locally predominant agriculture and reported higher soil carbon stocks, lower bulk density and higher species richness in several measured groups. The paper itself notes that scientific evidence on permaculture remains scarce.
   - https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-024-01405-8

3. **Bec Hellouin circulation/work example.** INRAE describes the Bec Hellouin study and explicitly notes that reorganising the Mandala garden from a spiral to rays had a strong work-time effect. INRAE also warns against treating one singular farm as universally representative.
   - https://www.inrae.fr/actualites/ferme-du-bec-hellouin-beaute-rend-productif

4. **Cohousing benefits and burdens.** Maxwell Watts et al. (2026), *Journal of Community Psychology*, reports meaning, support and security together with social burden, conflict and distress in a self-governing cohousing community.
   - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13077417/

5. **Visible and invisible household work.** Ledent and Salembier (2021), *Buildings*, examines how collective visibility can make household work easier to deliberate while mental load can remain a material issue.
   - https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/11/5/189

6. **Nature-based-solution implementation gap.** A 2025 review in *Journal of Environmental Management* identifies governance, expertise, evidence, stakeholder conflict and finance as material barriers/enablers, with co-design and polycentric governance among the potential enablers.
   - https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301479725019838

7. **Satoyama.** Japan's Ministry of the Environment describes sustainable management of natural resources in socio-ecological production landscapes under the Satoyama Initiative.
   - https://www.env.go.jp/nature/satoyama/syuhourei/practices_en.html

8. **Participatory permaculture toolkit.** Malhotra et al. (2025/2026 issue), *The Design Journal*, describes a toolkit iteratively co-designed with farmers over two years using visual aids, interactive models, mapping and repeated co-creation workshops.
   - https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14606925.2025.2573718

9. **Korean community-village sustainability.** Park and Lee (2018) organize sustainability guidance around social environment, maintenance/management, regional environment and economic environment, including resident participation, decision-making and operational responsibility.
   - https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART002347566

10. **Temperate food forests.** Knook et al. (2025), *Agroforestry Systems*, describes a young and fragmented research field and calls for more work on mature systems, harvesting, economics and revenue models.
    - https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10457-025-01327-0

The German Permakultur Institut is used as a practitioner/design-process source, not as scientific proof:
- https://www.permakultur.de/was-ist-permakultur

## Claim boundary and counter-read

### What the article may say

- permaculture provides design concepts worth testing against Økotun;
- multilingual searching changes which meanings and adjacent practices become visible;
- cohousing evidence contains both potential benefit and burden;
- hidden labour, maintenance and governance are legitimate design variables;
- multifunctionality needs operational and failure-boundary controls;
- co-design does not imply compulsory resident operation;
- the next meaningful Økotun test is a concrete site scenario.

### What the article does not say

- permaculture proves Økotun;
- the cited farms or cohousing communities are equivalent to Økotun;
- Økotun will improve health, biodiversity, affordability or community outcomes;
- approximately 100 homes is an empirically optimal scale;
- any Norwegian site is feasible;
- residents should be required to perform community work;
- the project has regulatory, financial, market or municipal approval.

### Strongest counterargument preserved

Most of the desirable properties described here can also be pursued through ordinary good housing, landscape architecture, cooperative governance and professional facilities management without adopting the label or full tradition of permaculture. The article therefore treats permaculture as a design lens and search seed, not as a necessary identity or causal explanation.
