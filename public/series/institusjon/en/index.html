New series · Institutions

Institution — power, knowledge and the state

What do we actually preserve when we preserve an institution? The series began with the history of a word and grew into an investigation of how societies make knowledge, legal safeguards, governance and criticism durable — without making institutions untouchable.

From word to social function

More than the building and the organisation

The word “institution” can refer to an arrangement, an organisation and a physical place. The series examines what happens when these no longer coincide: when the structure remains but the function weakens — or when the function continues in a new form.

Power and boundaries

Who can instruct whom?

For each body, we go beyond phrases such as “can set frameworks” and “can set objectives”. We examine which frameworks, which objectives, who can set them, what is protected, where the grey zones lie, and who controls the boundaries.

Questions we are investigating

History → legal basis → practice → accountability

Universities

Who educates the state?

Public universities are part of the state, yet parts of their academic activity are legally protected from instruction. The series examines which frameworks the state can actually set, and which forms of power universities exercise through knowledge, degrees, professions and academic authority.

Oversight and counter-power

Institutions that must be able to challenge the state

The courts, the Office of the Auditor General, the Parliamentary Ombud, Statistics Norway, Norges Bank and the Norwegian National Human Rights Institution have very different forms of independence. We examine those differences rather than hiding them under one label.

Norges Bank

Setting objectives is not the same as issuing instructions

Who decides what monetary policy should seek to achieve, and who decides the instruments? Historical shifts, legal authority and contemporary debates provide a concrete test case.

Critical counter-track

Who holds the independent to account?

Autonomy is not an end in itself. The series examines how institutions can be free enough to correct power while keeping their own power open to scrutiny and correction.

Publication rhythm

Friday, when the evidence is ready

The series is ongoing. Friday is the regular publication day, not an obligation to fill a slot. A part is published when the sources, counterarguments, authority boundaries and language are mature enough.
