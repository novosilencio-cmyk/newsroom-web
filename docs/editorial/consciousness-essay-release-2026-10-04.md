## Comparison and semantic control

Decision: COMBINE. Preserve the selected Norwegian essay and the displayed afterword; add source-role/year clarification and full translations. The comparison rejects unsupported premises about leaders’ mental states. No threshold or ranking of who is conscious enough is created. A person can have a sound argument without full self-insight; the argument, not the speaker's status, must carry the conclusion. Commercial interest is not a refutation. Body sensation is not used as a physiological diagnosis.

Specific translation control: English distinguishes consciousness/awareness; French and Spanish retain the afterword's explicit distinction; Chinese and Japanese distinguish subjective experience from noticing learned interpretations; German and Portuguese preserve possibility rather than certainty; Korean preserves non-equivalence; Arabic distinguishes subjective experience from observing one's reactions, and keeps the financing condition and uncertainty about individual leaders. None received separate human native-language proofreading.

The Norwegian style exercise's article-scoped exception mechanism preserves the author-selected title and consequential negations, including the verbatim source title. Exact phrases, not a blanket language exemption, are recorded. Removing these negations would reverse or materially distort the approved meaning. The checker itself is unchanged.
