# Improvement & Cost-Shift Investigation — anchoring proposal 001

Status: CANDIDATE / INTERNAL / NOT CANONICAL / NO AUTHORITY EFFECT
Origin: Bjørn Moe Aldema

## Core candidate

When a policy, technology, efficiency measure, cut, reform or other change is presented as an improvement, investigate:

1. What is actually improved?
2. For whom?
3. Which valued function is preserved, strengthened or weakened?
4. Which present and future capacities are created, preserved, stranded or destroyed?
5. Are costs shifted to other people, institutions, regions, ecosystems or future generations?
6. Are benefits or harms distributed unevenly or hidden by aggregate measures?
7. Which alternative carriers could preserve the valued function with better total consequences?
8. What evidence would reverse the leading interpretation?

## Why this should not become a new subsystem

The candidate overlaps existing OØS capabilities:

- advisory investigation and challenge/counterevidence;
- broad-search/source control;
- policy-and-society, economy-and-finance and human-capacity domain adapters;
- Lost Option Accounting;
- functional carrier analysis;
- reserve and developmental-capacity distinctions;
- long-horizon and distributional reasoning.

Therefore the preferred architecture is a thin cross-project prompt/control that routes into existing capabilities when triggered.

## Proposed cross-project anchoring

### Layer A — OØS common candidate

Candidate role: reusable analytical prompt for consequential analysis and advisory work.

Recommended integration point after validation:

`OOS_ADVISORY_FUNCTION_STANDARD_001`

as a cross-domain check after evidence gathering / before recommendation filtering, with activation when the object involves one or more of:

- efficiency or cost-cutting;
- policy reform;
- technology/material substitution;
- resource allocation;
- welfare/support design;
- environmental or intergenerational trade-offs;
- institutional restructuring;
- claims of improvement that may externalise costs.

Do not run it mechanically on trivial work.

### Layer B — journalism / newsroom adapter

Journalistic form:

`CLAIMED IMPROVEMENT -> FUNCTION -> WHO GAINS/LOSES -> CAPACITY EFFECT -> COST SHIFT -> TIME HORIZON -> ALTERNATIVE CARRIERS -> COUNTEREVIDENCE -> ACCOUNTABILITY`

Use as an investigation lens, not as a predetermined editorial conclusion.

### Layer C — state/nation advisory adapter

For policy-support or country requests:

`MANDATE/GOAL -> CURRENT FUNCTION -> CURRENT CARRIER -> CURRENT COST -> CAPACITY CREATED/LOST -> DISTRIBUTIONAL EFFECT -> ECOLOGICAL/FUTURE EFFECT -> LOCAL ALTERNATIVES -> IMPLEMENTATION CAPACITY -> DECISION/AUTHORITY OWNER -> OUTCOME CHECK`

This adapter should connect to local evidence, law, institutions, culture and implementation reality before any recommendation.

### Layer D — domain-specific extension

Examples:

- materials/production: toxicity, persistence, repairability, end-of-life, accumulation;
- welfare: safety net, capacity restoration, social investment, role-modelling, initiative-enabling security;
- education: developmental capacity, pipelines, option loss, work/application capacity;
- housing: enabling infrastructure, affordability, life-course effects, local productive capacity;
- transport: mobility function, pollution/toxicity, accessibility, local economic connection;
- nature/resource policy: ecosystem reserve, regeneration, irreversible loss, future option space.

## Trigger profile candidate

Activate proportionately when at least one is material:

- strong claim of efficiency/improvement;
- present saving with possible future cost;
- trade-off across people, places or generations;
- ecological externality;
- risk that aggregate gain hides concentrated harm;
- replacement of a functional carrier;
- policy that may reduce or restore human capacity;
- recommendation to a public authority.

## Negative controls

Do not infer that:

- every cost-cut is harmful;
- every investment creates future capacity;
- local/traditional/natural is automatically better;
- present wellbeing always outweighs ecological or future costs;
- future benefits justify unlimited present cost;
- more welfare/support is automatically better;
- analytical competence transfers democratic authority.

## Validation path

1. Argentina — first real journalism + state-advisory test.
2. Japan — transfer test in a different institutional/cultural setting.
3. South Korea — transfer test with housing/family/capacity dynamics.
4. One negative-control case where the lens should add little or conclude that the claimed efficiency is genuinely beneficial with no material shifted cost.
5. Only then consider cross-project canonical integration.

## External comparison signals — discovery only

Useful external parallels found in read-only research:

- New Zealand Treasury Living Standards Framework: systematic policy analysis across wellbeing, long-term and distributional implications, including human, natural, social and financial/physical capital.
- Wales Well-being of Future Generations framework: long-term, prevention, integration, collaboration and involvement as explicit public-sector ways of working.

These are comparative inspirations, not authority transfers or wholesale adoptions.

## Recommendation

Use the candidate now as a newsroom and Argentina advisory lens. Preserve it as a cross-project candidate. Do not canonise until transfer and negative-control tests show that it improves discrimination without creating excessive analysis burden.
